Notes

  • Scale ratings are the author's qualitative judgments of how much each provision could change US emissions. No one has modeled the full bill; the one published run (C2ES/Greenline) covers only transmission and interconnection in four regions. Other numbers are context anchors, not estimates of the bill's effect.
  • NEPA row: clean projects outnumber fossil in federal environmental reviews, but an emissions-weighted recount of 2010-18 energy EISs (preliminary, not yet externally reviewed) finds the fossil projects carry more emissions, driven by gas pipelines and coal leases. That sample predates offshore wind and today's large transmission proposals, so the row is rated 'either way' rather than 'raises'.
  • Sec. 1401 appears twice. Its protection of issued permits currently shields wind and solar, but legal analysts warn its broad wording could also hinder future pollution rules for operating facilities. The manager's amendment may change it.
  • 'Helps' reflects who faces federal obstacles and most NEPA reviews today; neutral provisions can protect either side under future administrations.
  • Offshore wind anchor assumes a 40-45% capacity factor and 0.35-0.50 tCO2/MWh displaced. For scale, plaintiffs' consultants (Charles River Associates) put 57 GW of wind, solar and hybrid capacity as canceled or delayed by federal actions, 44-88 MtCO2/yr if built (25-35% capacity factor). Most of it awaits approvals, which the bill's deadlines force decisions on but do not guarantee, so that range is what is at stake, not the bill's effect.
  • Also in the bill: faster Endangered Species Act consultations and limits on challenges (secs. 1302-1305), which cut both ways.
  • Not in this bill (unlike the 2024 Manchin-Barrasso bill): mandatory oil and gas lease sales, coal leasing, or an LNG-specific export deadline (general permit clocks could still reach DOE). The July 2025 budget law already mandated oil, gas and coal leasing; DOE lifted the LNG export pause in Jan. 2025.
